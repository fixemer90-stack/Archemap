## Self D7 publication time

- The `basic_report_ready` event and outbox row commit with the usable Self foundation, but `occurred_at=report.created_at` is the PostgreSQL transaction-start timestamp. Joint visibility does not prove that `occurred_at` is when the foundation became usable. This affects D7, not the atomic event/outbox guarantee.
- Disposable PostgreSQL reproduced the issue through the real Self producer and `build_overview`. The on-time control activated. In the late case a separate reader could not see the report at or after the strict deadline while the transaction was still open; a fresh owner-scoped read after commit found the deterministic foundation and one readiness event/outbox pair. Overview nevertheless certified one activation instead of withholding the metric. A test-only replacement with `clock_timestamp()` immediately before emission reproduced the same false activation. LLM was disabled and external export/delivery was off.
- An immutable event written in the foundation transaction cannot contain a timestamp obtained after that transaction becomes visible. PostgreSQL commit metadata is a database-recorded timestamp, not proof that a fresh reader could see the artifact at that instant; it is not retained indefinitely and is not available for commits made without tracking. Conversely, a new transaction that successfully reads the foundation supplies only an upper bound. A process exit after the foundation commit can lose an in-memory observation, and a retry must not assign its current time to the original publication or rerun generation.
- The conservative implementation stages an event/report/owner/generation-bound intent and a PostgreSQL wall-clock lower bound in a savepoint of the original foundation transaction. This is evidence that the new foundation cannot have been visible earlier, not a commit timestamp. After commit, a separate transaction reads the exact owned, usable report and records an upper bound once. A proof-only write failure does not undo the foundation or change narrative progression; the atomic fact/outbox write retains its existing rollback behavior.
- The existing Self reconciler retries pending observations even for terminal jobs. Recovery reads committed intent and uses the real observation time, never a backdated timestamp. A missing intent is not reconstructed, and duplicates/reuse cannot start new proof or rerun generation on behalf of analytics. The migration is additive and does not backfill. See [S04](./S04-business-overview-api.md#self-d7-timing-coverage).

- Disposable PostgreSQL/Redis: two expected REDs with one on-time control, then 70 passing producer, overview, outbox and migration cases. PostgreSQL rejected intent and observation writes in separate scenarios without blocking an owner-scoped foundation read or narrative completion. Reconciliation recovered pending proof after a terminal report, preserving the original report, fact and outbox; an observation at cutoff remained unavailable. No live providers or production services were used.
- Independent-review follow-up: the proof savepoint caps PostgreSQL statement and lock waits and restores caller settings before release. A PostgreSQL INSERT trigger blocked on an advisory lock; a fresh connection could read the committed foundation, fact and outbox while the lock remained held. Migration `e23d7b001` protects the original identity and lower bound from edits, permits a single pending-to-observed transition, and rejects deletion or truncation. The observation still requires a fresh matching source; once finalized, proof survives source-report deletion. The immediate-predecessor upgrade preserves legacy readiness and its outbox without creating proof.
